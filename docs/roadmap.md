# Roadmap

## Overview

ACTA is developed around a simple principle:

> Grow only when growth strengthens the philosophy.

The goal is not to become the largest productivity platform or compete with enterprise software.

ACTA exists to provide a focused, lightweight, and sustainable productivity experience built around meaningful actions.

Every milestone should strengthen that vision—not replace it.

---

## Development Philosophy

ACTA follows an iterative development process.

Each release is expected to solve meaningful problems rather than increase feature count.

New functionality is introduced only when it:

- Reduces friction
- Improves clarity
- Strengthens the action-first workflow
- Improves long-term maintainability
- Enhances the overall user experience

Complexity is never considered a goal.

If a feature increases complexity without providing meaningful value, it does not belong in ACTA.

The roadmap is therefore guided by philosophy rather than functionality alone.

---

## Current Development Phase

ACTA is currently in the **Platform Refinement Phase**.

The project's identity, architecture, and core systems have already been established.

The challenge is no longer discovering what ACTA should become.

The challenge is refining what ACTA already is.

Future development focuses primarily on:

- Stability
- Consistency
- Synchronization
- Performance
- Long-term maintainability

Rather than introducing major conceptual changes.

---

## Current Foundation

The platform already includes its core systems:

- Action Logging
- AP and Level Progression
- History Tracking
- Statistics
- Daily Records
- Streak Tracking
- Achievement Systems
- Todo Integration
- User Profiles
- ACTA Cards
- Import and Export
- Local-First Storage
- Cloud Synchronization Infrastructure

These systems form the foundation of all future development.

The primary goal moving forward is refinement rather than reinvention.

---

## Near-Term Priorities

### Synchronization Improvements

Synchronization remains one of the most important areas of development.

Future work includes:

- More reliable cloud persistence
- Improved account transitions
- Better recovery workflows
- Reduced synchronization friction
- Improved multi-device continuity

The objective is seamless synchronization without compromising user ownership.

---

### Mobile Experience

Mobile usability remains a high priority.

Focus areas include:

- Responsive layouts
- Better toolbar organization
- Improved touch interactions
- Keyboard behavior optimization
- Consistent spacing and hierarchy
- Reduced visual clutter

The goal is to provide a first-class experience on both desktop and mobile devices.

---

### User Experience Refinement

Many future updates will focus on reducing friction throughout the platform.

Examples include:

- Cleaner workflows
- Faster interactions
- Better discoverability
- Improved feedback systems
- More intuitive navigation
- Increased visual consistency

Small improvements often create the largest long-term benefits.

---

### Architectural Refinement

As ACTA grows, maintaining architectural clarity becomes increasingly important.

Future work may include:

- Domain separation improvements
- Internal system cleanup
- Performance optimization
- Better maintainability
- Improved scalability

The goal is sustainable growth without unnecessary complexity.

---

## Cross-Device Experience

ACTA is designed around the idea that productivity should continue seamlessly across devices.

Users should be able to move between environments without disrupting their workflow.

Synchronization exists to support continuity—not dependency.

Data ownership remains a core principle.

Export and backup mechanisms will continue to exist regardless of future cloud capabilities.

---

## Platform Strategy

ACTA is fundamentally a web-first product.

The web remains the primary platform because it provides:

- Accessibility
- Simplicity
- Broad compatibility
- Low maintenance overhead

Progressive Web App (PWA) support already enables an application-like experience while preserving the flexibility of the web.

Native applications may become possible in the future, but they are not current priorities.

The focus remains on making the web experience exceptional before expanding elsewhere.

---

## Cloud Strategy

Cloud functionality exists to improve accessibility—not to replace ownership.

Users should always retain control over their data.

ACTA follows a local-first philosophy:

- Local usage remains fully supported
- Cloud synchronization remains optional
- Import and export remain available
- User data remains portable

Cloud services should complement the platform rather than define it.

---

## Community

ACTA is designed primarily for individual productivity.

Collaboration is intentionally outside the project's core scope.

However, lightweight community-oriented features may gradually appear when they naturally support motivation and reflection.

Possible examples include:

- Public Profiles
- ACTA Cards
- Achievement Sharing
- Exportable Reports
- Progress Summaries

The goal is inspiration and reflection—not social competition.

---

## Open Source

ACTA is developed as an open-source project.

Open development encourages:

- Transparency
- Learning
- Experimentation
- Community contribution

The project exists as both a personal journey and an educational project.

Open source reflects that spirit.

---

## What ACTA Is Not Trying to Become

Some commonly requested productivity features are intentionally not prioritized.

Examples include:

- Enterprise collaboration
- Workspace management
- Corporate productivity suites
- Complex project management systems
- Heavy automation
- Feature-heavy integrations
- Productivity systems driven primarily by AI

These capabilities may be valuable for other products, but they do not align with ACTA's philosophy.

Choosing what not to build is just as important as choosing what to build.

---

## Long-Term Vision

ACTA does not seek endless expansion.

The goal is not to accumulate features indefinitely.

Instead, ACTA aims to reach a point where its philosophy is fully realized.

Future development will continue refining the platform until additional complexity no longer creates meaningful value.

A finished product is not defined by feature count.

It is defined by clarity, consistency, and completeness.

---

## Success Criteria

The long-term success of ACTA will not be measured by:

- Number of features
- Team collaboration capabilities
- Enterprise adoption
- Ecosystem size
- User acquisition metrics alone

Instead, success will be measured by a simpler question:

> Does ACTA help people recognize meaningful progress through their actions?

If users can look back and clearly understand what they have accomplished over time, then ACTA has fulfilled its purpose.

---

## Looking Beyond the Roadmap

Technology will continue to evolve.

New opportunities will emerge.

ACTA remains open to future innovation, but every major decision will continue to be evaluated against a single question:

> Does this strengthen the philosophy, or merely increase complexity?

If the answer is the latter, the feature should wait—or never be built at all.

The roadmap is not a promise to build everything.

It is a commitment to protect the identity of the project while allowing it to mature.

---

## Closing Statement

ACTA is not trying to become everything.

It is trying to become one thing exceptionally well.

Rather than competing to offer the most features, the project focuses on delivering a thoughtful, consistent, and sustainable productivity experience centered around meaningful actions.

The roadmap reflects that commitment:

> Refine before expanding.  
> Simplify before adding.  
> Let philosophy guide development.

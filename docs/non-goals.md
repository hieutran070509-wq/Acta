# Non-Goals

> Software is defined as much by what it refuses to become as by what it chooses to build.

---

## Why This Document Exists

Every software project faces an endless number of possible features.

Without clear boundaries, products gradually become larger, more complicated, and less focused.

ACTA is intentionally designed with a limited scope.

This document describes what ACTA is **not** trying to become, ensuring that future development remains aligned with the project's philosophy and long-term direction.

Choosing not to build certain features is a deliberate design decision—not a limitation.

---

## Core Principle

ACTA exists to recognize meaningful actions and preserve progress over time.

Any feature that moves the product away from this purpose should be questioned carefully.

The goal is not to become a general productivity platform.

The goal is to remain focused on action recognition, reflection, and long-term consistency.

---

## What ACTA Is Not

### 1. Not a Traditional To-Do List

ACTA includes task management features, but tasks are not the center of the experience.

Traditional to-do applications focus primarily on future intentions.

ACTA focuses on completed actions.

Planning is supported when useful, but progress is ultimately measured by what has actually been done.

---

### 2. Not a Calendar Application

ACTA is not intended to replace calendars or scheduling software.

Users may organize upcoming work when necessary, but time management is not the primary purpose of the platform.

ACTA measures actions—not appointments.

---

### 3. Not a Project Management Tool

ACTA is designed primarily for individuals.

It does not aim to manage projects, assign responsibilities, track organizational workflows, or coordinate teams.

Dedicated project management platforms already solve these problems effectively.

ACTA intentionally focuses on personal productivity.

---

### 4. Not a Collaborative Workspace

Real-time collaboration is outside the project's primary scope.

Future versions may allow users to share progress cards, achievements, or public profiles.

However, ACTA is not intended to become a collaborative workspace.

Individual ownership remains the foundation of the experience.

---

### 5. Not an AI-Driven Productivity Assistant

Artificial intelligence continues to evolve rapidly, but ACTA is not designed around AI.

The platform emphasizes simple workflows, transparency, and user intention rather than automated decision-making.

Technology should support users—not replace their judgment.

If AI is introduced in the future, it should enhance existing workflows rather than define them.

---

### 6. Not a Social Network

ACTA is not built around followers, feeds, likes, rankings, or continuous social engagement.

Any future sharing functionality should exist to celebrate personal progress—not encourage comparison or competition.

The user's progress belongs primarily to the user.

---

### 7. Not a Feature Collection

ACTA does not aim to implement every productivity methodology.

Features are added only when they reinforce the project's philosophy.

Complexity without meaningful value is intentionally avoided.

A smaller and more coherent system is preferred over a larger but inconsistent one.

---

### 8. Not a Replacement for Everything

ACTA is not designed to replace every productivity application.

Different tools solve different problems.

Calendars, note-taking software, flashcard systems, project management platforms, and knowledge management tools each have their own strengths.

ACTA complements these tools by focusing on one specific problem:

**Recognizing meaningful actions and transforming them into measurable long-term progress.**

---

## Feature Evaluation Framework

Whenever a new feature is proposed, the following questions should be considered:

- Does it strengthen the action-recognition philosophy?
- Does it reduce friction rather than increase it?
- Does it keep the platform simple and focused?
- Does it help users better understand their own progress?
- Does it improve consistency across the product?

If the answer to most of these questions is no, the feature probably does not belong in ACTA.

---

## Historical Context

As ACTA evolved from its earliest terminal-inspired versions into a modern web application, many potential features were considered and intentionally rejected.

The project matured not only through what was added, but also through what was deliberately left out.

These boundaries helped preserve the product's identity while preventing unnecessary complexity.

---

## Closing Statement

ACTA is intentionally limited in scope.

These limitations are not weaknesses—they are part of the product's identity.

Rather than becoming an all-in-one productivity platform, ACTA aims to do one thing exceptionally well:

**Help people recognize, preserve, and reflect on the actions they actually take.**
